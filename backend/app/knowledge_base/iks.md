# Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS) & Paninian Grammar

## What is Nyaya logic?
Nyaya is one of the six classical schools (darshanas) of Indian philosophy, focused on
logic and epistemology (pramana-shastra). It defines valid means of knowledge —
perception (pratyaksha), inference (anumana), comparison (upamana), and testimony
(shabda) — and lays out a formal five-step syllogism (pratijna, hetu, udaharana,
upanaya, nigamana) used to structure a logical argument. Nyaya's rigorous treatment
of inference and fallacies has drawn comparison to Aristotelian logic and is an
active reference point in Indian computational logic and AI-reasoning research.

## Paninian Grammar
Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī, composed around the 5th century BCE, is a highly systematic
generative grammar of Sanskrit built from about 4,000 sutras (rules) organized with
meta-rules for ordering and rule conflict resolution. Its karaka system describes
semantic roles (agent, object, instrument, location, etc.) independent of surface
case marking, which modern computational linguists have used as a framework for
building semantic role labelling and dependency parsers for Sanskrit and other
Indic languages. Panini's rule-based, algorithmic approach is frequently cited as
a historical precursor to formal grammar notations like Backus-Naur Form (BNF)
used in programming language design.

## Why IKS matters for modern computing
Indian Knowledge Systems research is not purely historical — it actively informs
present-day NLP: karaka-based parsing for Sanskrit, Nyaya-inspired formal
reasoning systems, and Ayurveda/Jyotisha data digitization projects are areas
where classical frameworks are being encoded into modern software and datasets.
