# Bhashini & Indian Language Technology

## What is Bhashini?
Bhashini is India's national mission platform for language technology,
intended to make speech-to-text, machine translation, and text-to-speech
available as shared infrastructure across Indian languages. It aggregates
open datasets and models contributed by research institutions and startups,
and exposes them through common APIs so any developer or government service
can add multilingual capability — for example, a form that reads its
questions aloud in a citizen's chosen language, or a chatbot that answers in
regional languages — without training models from scratch.

## Core capabilities
- Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) tuned for Indian accents and code-mixing
  (e.g., Hindi-English "Hinglish").
- Neural Machine Translation (NMT) across scheduled Indian languages and
  English.
- Text-to-Speech (TTS) with natural-sounding voices for multiple languages.
- OCR support for regional scripts to digitize printed and handwritten text.

## Low-resource language challenges
Languages such as Santali, Bodo, or Meitei Mayek have far less digitized text
and speech data than Hindi, Bengali, or Tamil, which makes model training
harder and evaluation less reliable. Community-sourced data collection,
transfer learning from related languages, and script-aware tokenization are
common strategies used to close this gap incrementally rather than all at
once.

## Why it matters for public services
Multilingual voice and translation interfaces reduce the literacy and
language barrier for citizens accessing welfare schemes, grievance redress
portals, and health information — letting a service be usable in a person's
first language rather than requiring English or Hindi fluency.
