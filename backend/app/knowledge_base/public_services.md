# Digital Public Infrastructure & Public Services

## What is India's DPI stack?
India's Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) approach is often described as
layered, interoperable "digital rails" built as public goods rather than a
single monolithic system: a digital identity layer (Aadhaar) for unique
identification, a payments layer (Unified Payments Interface / UPI) for
real-time low-cost transactions, and a consent-based data-sharing layer
(Account Aggregator framework, based on DEPA — the Data Empowerment and
Protection Architecture) that lets citizens securely share their own
financial or other data with services they authorize.

## How DPI supports public services
Because these layers are shared and interoperable, individual government or
private services do not need to rebuild identity verification or payments
from scratch — a welfare disbursement scheme, a health record system, or a
municipal service portal can all plug into the same underlying rails,
reducing duplication and onboarding friction for citizens.

## Multilingual and inclusive access
Public service portals are increasingly pairing DPI rails with Bhashini-style
translation and voice interfaces so that services remain usable for citizens
who are not literate in English or the portal's default language.

## Open data & transparency
Platforms such as data.gov.in publish government datasets — budget
allocations, scheme beneficiary data, infrastructure statistics — for public
reuse, enabling civic-tech applications, journalism, and academic research
that track how public services are actually being delivered.
